About this system

What it does

This system suggests films you might like, based on how you and other viewers have rated films in the past. Tell it a viewer ID or a film you enjoyed, and it finds patterns in the ratings data to surface films you haven't seen yet. It doesn't look at plot, cast or genre to make its suggestions — only at rating patterns.

The data

The system is built on MovieLens 100K, a public ratings dataset from GroupLens Research at the University of Minnesota: {{ "{:,}".format(stats.raw_viewers) }} viewers, {{ "{:,}".format(stats.raw_films) }} films and {{ "{:,}".format(stats.raw_ratings) }} ratings, collected {{ stats.earliest_rating.strftime('%B %Y') }} to {{ stats.latest_rating.strftime('%B %Y') }}. Films rated by fewer than {{ min_ratings }} viewers are excluded, so there's enough rating history behind every recommendation.

The method

The system uses collaborative filtering: it recommends films from patterns in how viewers rate them, not from the films' own content. Two versions run side by side. User-based filtering compares your ratings with every other viewer's, using a measure called cosine similarity to find the viewers whose taste is closest to yours, then recommends the films they rated highly. Item-based filtering compares films the same way — by who rated them alike — and recommends films with a rating pattern similar to one you name. Both look only at a handful of the closest matches rather than the whole dataset, a technique called k-nearest neighbours.

Where it falls short

  • A new viewer with no ratings yet can't be matched to anyone — there's nothing to compare.
  • Most viewers have rated only a small share of the catalogue, so many comparisons rest on limited overlap.
  • Recommendation scores are summed across neighbours, so films liked a little by many neighbours can outrank films loved deeply by a few.
  • The catalogue is MovieLens 100K, which ends in 1998 — nothing more recent is in the system.
  • The dataset lists Chasing Amy (1997) twice under two IDs, so it appears twice with its ID shown to tell the entries apart.
